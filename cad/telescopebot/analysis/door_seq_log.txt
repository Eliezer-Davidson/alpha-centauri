==============================================================================
(e) SPINE POSITION DURING THE PRY
==============================================================================
CLAIM AS WRITTEN ('spine must be DOWN so the load path goes through the
chassis') is CONFIRMED -- but the usual reason given for it is WRONG.

1. STATIC tipping is INDEPENDENT of CG height.
   Overturning = F*h_force. Restoring = W*d. Neither contains z_cg.
   (CAD-DECISION.md sec 4(f) says the same thing for the humanoid.)
   => 'spine up raises the CG so it tips more' is FALSE for a static pull.

2. What DOES change, #1: THE HEIGHT AT WHICH THE FORCE IS APPLIED.
   chassis hook, spine down         h=0.10 m  M = 25.0*0.10 =   2.50 N.m  vs budget 4.59 -> PASS
   arm at the lever, spine up       h=1.05 m  M = 25.0*1.05 =  26.25 N.m  vs budget 4.59 -> TIPS
   ratio = 10.5x. THIS is the quantified difference, and it is
   5.7x over the budget at the top. Not marginal -- catastrophic.

3. What DOES change, #2: INERTIAL overturning when the door breaks free.
   Model it as an ELASTIC RELEASE, not a constant-force acceleration: the leg
   servos are POSITION-controlled, so when the door lets go the only stored
   energy is the elastic + backlash deflection accumulated at F_WORST.
   shank bending at 25 N = 0.24 mm; hip backlash 0.43 deg (robonine measured) x 0.24 m = 1.80 mm -> s = 2.04 mm
   E = 0.5*F*s = 25.5 mJ -> v = sqrt(2E/m) = 0.096 m/s -> a = v^2/(2s) = 2.26 m/s^2
   spine DOWN       moving 1.66 kg at z=0.20, fixed 3.87 kg at z=0.18 -> z_cg = 0.186 m
                    M_inertial = m*a*z_cg = 2.32 N.m vs budget 4.59 -> PASS
   spine UP 250 mm  moving 1.66 kg at z=0.45, fixed 3.87 kg at z=0.18 -> z_cg = 0.261 m
                    M_inertial = m*a*z_cg = 3.26 N.m vs budget 4.59 -> MARGINAL
   spine up is 1.40x worse inertially (40%).
   The snap is survivable BECAUSE the actuators are stiff position sources with
   almost no stored energy. It stops being survivable the moment anything
   compliant is added to the load path (a spring-loaded paw, a soft hook mount,
   a long TPU pad). DESIGN RULE: the door-pull load path must be STIFF.

4. What DOES change, #3: MAST BENDING. Two M8x1.25 rods, 120 mm apart,
   bending in the SAGITTAL plane -> each rod bends about its OWN axis;
   the 120 mm spacing buys nothing for fore-aft loads.
   M8x1.25 minor dia 6.647 mm -> I = pi*d^4/64 = 95.8 mm^4/rod, Z = 28.8 mm^3
   cantilever L=250 mm, F=25.0 N -> M_base 6.25 N.m
     stress/rod = 108 MPa (grade 4.8 rod yield ~320 MPa, SF 3.0x -- but every thread root is a notch)
     sway = F L^3/(3 E I_total) = 3.40 mm at the chest
   spine DOWN: mast bending = 0. Force goes straight into the chassis.

VERDICT: CONFIRMED, restated. The rule is not 'spine down because the CG is
lower'. The rule is: THE PULLING FORCE MUST BE APPLIED LOW AND THROUGH THE
CHASSIS. Spine-down is a consequence of that, plus it deletes 3.4 mm of mast
sway and 40% of the inertial overturning.
