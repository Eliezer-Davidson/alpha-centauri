  to ~245 mm. (Bambu wiki/store, verified 2026-08-27.)

  (ALPHA-CENTAURI-PLAN §7, §5.1) is STALE — it describes the previous laptop's
  era. Current inventory:
  **six DSSERVO DS3235SG 180°** (PWM, no feedback, 8.4 V max), currently

- **Battery voltage law**: the nominal 600 g "6S-class" pack (25.2 V) in

## 1. Fabrication on the A1 mini

Most parts already fit: thigh (146 upright), calf (~165 on its side), foot,
hip modules, joint parts, chest box (150×130×85), waist box, towers. The spine
verticals are steel rod / T8 screw, not prints.

Casualties and fixes:

| Part | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Chassis half 250×160 | no orientation fits | `CHASSIS_SPLIT_COUNT` 2 → 4 (125 mm quarters) + bolted splice with shear keys; splices land at x=±125, away from pods and the rack well |
| Dovetail ways 250 ×4 | too long | segment at 125, joints on the chassis splice lines |
| Carriage 200×170 | too big | two bolted mirror halves, dowelled |
| Spine ratchet rack 288×24 (STROKE 250 + ENGAGE 40 — the "~250" here was the stroke, not the part) | exceeds even the 245 diagonal | two segments on a tooth-pitch boundary at z = 144 (DONE 2026-09-07, `spine_lock.py`). The "trim stroke to 245" fallback was mis-specified: a 245 mm PART caps the stroke at 245 − 40 ≈ 204 mm, not 245 |
| Foot (PETG core + TPU tread) | co-print designed for the H2C's SECOND nozzle | separate TPU tread part, mechanical retention (circumferential groove/flange) + flexible adhesive (Shoe Goo class); never glue alone |

Every new joint/splice gets the standard treatment: partcheck in print
orientation + swept-interference asserts where it moves.

**Materials** (replaces `MATERIAL_STRUCTURAL = "PC or PA-CF"`, which required
the H2C chamber — Bambu explicitly does not recommend ABS/ASA/PC/PA/PET-CF on
the open-frame A1 series):

- `MATERIAL_STRUCTURAL` → **PETG-CF** (servo pockets, knee brackets): HDT 68 °C,
  Vicat 85 °C, ~2× PETG notched impact, low-warp formulation, printable on A1
  mini with the hardened-steel 0.4 hotend (~$14 swap unit).
- Second thermal defense the H2C plan never had: bus servos have a WRITABLE
  over-temperature cutoff (stock ~70 °C) — set it to ~55–60 °C so the servo
  protects itself before any printed pocket creeps.
- `MATERIAL_BULK` stays PETG; `MATERIAL_GRIP` stays TPU 95A — **external spool
  only** (AMS lite cannot feed flexible TPU); A1 mini's direct drive handles it.
- PLA only for jigs/fit tests (creep ceiling ~35–40 °C under sustained load).

**Filament order** (region TBD — the author to name the country for a concrete cart):
2× PETG (Sunlu/eSUN class), 1× PETG-CF, 1× TPU 95A, 1× PLA, hardened-steel A1
hotend 0.4, dry storage. ≈ $110–125 at US street prices.

## 2. Brain: old Android phone

Pattern proven by OpenBot (Intel, ICRA 2021). Architecture:

    phone (vision, planning, UI, IMU, Wi-Fi)
      └─ USB-OTG → FE-URT-1 bus adapter (~$8, CH340 @ 1 Mbps — Android
         driver verified to support this baud)
           └─ one half-duplex TTL daisy chain → all 12–15 bus servos
    ESP32 (owned): hardware E-stop/watchdog + power supervisor only

- Servo I/O via FEETECH sync-write/sync-read — one packet for all servos per
  50 Hz tick (Android USB transactions cost 1–4 ms; never poll per-servo).
- Software path: small app on `usb-serial-for-android` (or usbserial4a
  Python-APK). **Termux cannot do USB serial** (no tty; known limitation) —
  keep Termux for SSH/dev only.
- Docking vision: ArUco is in core OpenCV ≥4.7 objdetect — standard Android
  SDK, no contrib build.
- Phone floor: Android 9–10, 3–4 GB RAM, ~2018+ 64-bit SoC. 24/7 power care:
  80–85 % charge limit, 5 V buck from the pack.
- Mass: replaces the 300 g `JETSON_ETC` budget with ~215 g (phone + adapter +
  buck) — sim E4 electronics-placement A/B remains valid, margins improve.

## 3. Power, self-charging, self battery-swap

Two **3S 18650 packs** (steel-can cells tolerate being mechanically handled
every cycle; 12.6 V full charge = exactly the servo spec), each behind an
ideal-diode module (~$5) into a common bus with bulk low-ESR capacitance.
A separate ~7.4 V buck rail serves the DS3235SG arms later (8.4 V-max servos
must never see the 3S rail directly).

Staged, each stage gated:

- **S0 — dual-pack electrics.** No mechanism. Gate: pull either pack under
  full servo load; no brownout, no reset.
- **S1 — self-docking recharge.** Printed funnel + V-rail dock, Roomba-pattern
  sprung blade contacts with wipe; dumb 12.6 V CC/CV brick in the dock;
  charging brains ON THE ROBOT (3S balance BMS + CC/CV board, ~$12); phone
  camera servoing onto an ArUco tag. Unattended-lithium stack: ≤0.5 C,
  redundant cutoffs (charger CV + BMS + timer), fireproof surface.
  Gate: robot docks itself from ~1 m and charges to cutoff, 5/5 attempts.
- **S2 — self battery-swap.** Station does the mechanical work (rack-pinion
  pusher, one cheap servo); robot side stays a passive sprung latch the
  station depresses. Robot rides through on the other pack. S2a manual
  station first, S2b motorized. Prior art at this scale: Wu et al. 2012
  (84 s cassette swap, ±8 mm capture tolerance from a passive funnel);
  above it, Atlas and Walker S2.

Stability check (sim stand data): removing one 300 g pack at x=−190 shifts
the parked CoM +12 to +15 mm against ~200–250 mm of static margin — swap
while standing is a stability non-event. Swap and dock always happen parked,
in the WALK station.

## 4. Servos

Measured demand (sim, per leg joint): stand 5.6 kg·cm worst; WALK crawl peak
**14.0 kg·cm** (abduction), 13.1 knees, 7.8 hips; WORK crawl demands the full
25 (and fails on polygon grounds regardless). Real sustained capability:
ST3215 ≈ 15 kg·cm (1.07× — no headroom), STS3250 ≈ 25 (1.8×), DS3235SG ≈
18–20 but PWM-blind and a different (40×20) footprint. The 35/50 kg·cm
datasheet numbers are stall fictions — the ~50 % law held on every servo
checked.

**Unblocking order (~$80): 1× ST3215 + 1× STS3250 + FE-URT-1.** It unblocks:

1. **The caliper** — measure both bodies in hand, resolve
   `SERVO_BODY_HEIGHT` (35.00 vs 37.80), unfreeze every servo-pocket print.
2. **The bench** — clamp servo, printed 100 mm lever on the horn, tip pressing
   a kitchen scale; ESP32 commands hold; scale reads live torque while the bus
   logs temperature for 90 min. Pass bar: holds ≥1.5 kg at the tip
   (≥15 kg·cm) without tripping. Bench the DS3235SG the same day for the
   honest ARM spec (drive it at 7.4–8.4 V via PWM).
3. **The control chain** — phone → OTG → adapter → servo proven on one unit.

**Fleet decision tree** (after the bench): ST3215 holds honestly →
12× ST3215 (~$300), pockets sized so the 8 high-load sockets (abductions +
knees) accept STS3250 upgrades without reprinting. ST3215 sags →
8× STS3250 + 4× ST3215 hips (~$460).

**Mk1 drive axes**: T8 lift (~31 turns) and rail pinion (~2–3 turns) require
bus servos in wheel/multi-turn mode — no 180° servo can ever drive them; the
±7-turn absolute window means the lift needs host-side turn counting + a
homing switch. ST3215s suffice there (lead screw is force-multiplied).
**Chest yaw** (still undrawn): bound it ≤180° and one DS3235SG can drive it.
**Arms**: the six DS3235SGs, 3 DOF each, when their current project releases
them — real ~18 kg·cm clears the plan's 10.8–12 kg·cm shoulder requirement
that the MG996R failed.
